About Hina

Born in Lahore in 1984, Hina Malik is a visual artist and educator based in Lahore. She graduated from Lahore College for Women University in 2003 and completed an M.Phil in Visual Arts with distinction from the College of Art & Design, University of the Punjab, in 2020, majoring in studio practice. She is currently pursuing a Master of Art Education at Beaconhouse National University.

Malik is an Assistant Professor and Head of the Fine Art Department at the Institute for Art and Culture. Her work moves between artistic practice, research, and education, with a sustained interest in questions of gender, cultural identity, domesticity, and the female body.

“The home is not merely a setting, but a vessel that contains, shapes, and can also confine identity.”

— Hina Malik, artist statement

Across painting, mixed media, and installation, Malik constructs interiors as psychological and cultural spaces. Furniture, rooms, fabric, doorways, and the traces of human presence become carriers of memory and tension, revealing how the familiar can hold both comfort and constraint.

The Silence of Spaces

Hina Malik’s practice begins inside the home, but its concerns extend far beyond architecture. The domestic interior becomes a metaphor for the cultural structures that surround the female body: spaces that nurture, protect, organise, and at times restrict the formation of an individual identity.

Her paintings often place bodies, garments, furniture, and rooms in close conversation. These elements appear intimate and recognisable, yet they also carry a sense of psychological distance. A chair, a bed, a doorway, or an emptied room can become a record of presence and absence, suggesting what has been lived, inherited, concealed, or left unsaid.

In works such as Can You Unsee, Love or Despair, and Should It Outshine My Silence, the figure and the interior are not separate subjects. They shape one another. Malik uses this relationship to investigate how female individuality is negotiated within cultural expectations and how identity can be continuously formed inside spaces traditionally associated with safety and belonging.

Her wider The Silence of Spaces series carries this inquiry into smaller, concentrated studies of interiors and objects. The scale may shift, but the tension remains: between intimacy and confinement, beauty and unease, preservation and change.

Artist Statement

Malik’s research-driven practice explores the relationship between the body and cultural identity, with particular attention to female individuality within the complex structures of culture. The interior of the home functions as a primary metaphor: not simply as a setting, but as a symbolic vessel that contains, shapes, and can confine identity.

Through carefully constructed interiors, she reflects on the paradox of domestic space. The same environment that is expected to nurture and protect can also become the place where a woman’s singular identity is negotiated, compromised, and relentlessly forged. Her work holds this contradiction in view, using the language of painting and installation to examine the emotional, cultural, and psychological weight of space.
